# Editorial Review — Biomedical Signal Processing and Control (BSPC)

**Role**: senior corresponding author / associate editor pass. Read the full repository before writing anything below: `README.md`, `SPEC.md`, `REPOSITORY_MAP.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, all of `docs/` (`experiment_contract.md`, `open_questions.md`, `geometry_metric_audit.md`, `threats_to_validity.md`, `internal_review.md`, `fisher_ratio_defense.md`, `pipeline_readiness.md`, `environment_requirements.md`), every CSV in `results/`, the primary figures in `figures/`, and the analysis/extraction scripts that produced them.

**Load-bearing fact this review opens with, because it changes what "review" means here**: **there is no manuscript.** `abstract.tex` (one paragraph, just drafted) is the only prose artifact resembling a submission. There is no Introduction, no Methods, no Results narrative, no Discussion, no bibliography, no figure captions written for a reader who hasn't lived inside this repository. What follows is therefore a review of *the underlying research package* — is the evidence real, is it sufficient, where would a BSPC reviewer attack — not a review of argumentation, framing, or prose, because none of that exists yet to review. Several of the 12 items below have to be answered as "not yet assessable" for exactly this reason, and I say so rather than inventing a verdict a manuscript hasn't earned yet.

## 1. One-sentence summary

A dose-response study of domain-adversarial disentanglement training in skin-lesion classification finds that increasing orthogonality regularization changes representation geometry substantially, that this geometric change does not improve (and does not correlate with) Mahalanobis-distance OOD/reliability estimation — which stays flat and *below* chance throughout — that this failure is shared by two other, structurally unrelated distance-based scorers (cosine-to-centroid, pooled k-NN), and that a supervised probe on the same embeddings nonetheless decodes domain membership well above chance, suggesting the representation retains domain information the entire tested family of distance-based scorers cannot access.

## 2. Main scientific claim

Within one fixed architecture and one fixed 16-d representation (`z_lesion`, CSG-Lite), varying only λ_orth: (a) representation geometry changes by two orders of magnitude on condition number (τ=0.84, exact p=2.8×10⁻⁵); (b) this change has no detectable association with Mahalanobis AUROC (all 5 geometry metrics, |τ|≤0.28, p>0.19 against AUROC); (c) AUROC itself stays flat at ~0.40 (below chance) across every λ_orth level; (d) this null result is not an artifact of Mahalanobis specifically — cosine-to-centroid and pooled k-NN (k=1/10/50) all land in the same ~0.41–0.42 band; (e) a supervised probe (logistic regression, linear SVM, random forest — none seeing the training objective) decodes ISIC-vs-PAD-UFES domain membership from the identical embeddings at ~0.73–0.81 AUROC, at every λ_orth level tested. The claim, stated at the evidence's actual strength: **domain information is not erased by this training family, but becomes inaccessible to distance-based geometry specifically.**

## 3. Core novelty

Not Mahalanobis, not OOD detection, not skin lesions. The novelty on offer is a dissociation between two properties of a learned representation that OOD/reliability literature routinely conflates: **decodability** (is the information present, extractable by a supervised readout) and **distance accessibility** (is the information usable by an unsupervised, geometry-based scorer). If this dissociation is real and general — not an artifact specific to this dataset pair or this training procedure — it is a genuinely useful, exportable finding for anyone building distance-based reliability/OOD estimators on top of adversarially-regularized representations. Whether it clears that bar is exactly what items 5–6 below test.

## 5. Claims that are unsupported

1. **"Domain information moved to another direction/subspace/manifold."** No direction, angle, subspace, or PCA analysis exists anywhere in `results/`. To the project's credit, this exact phrasing was proposed and then explicitly retracted in the working session — but it's exactly the sentence that will reappear under first-draft time pressure unless someone is watching for it. Flagging it here as a permanent tripwire, not a hypothetical.
2. **"The failure is specific to the disentangled representation, not a general property of domain shift"** (the baseline_soft causal reading). Confounded three ways simultaneously — architecture (ResNet-50 vs. CSG-Lite), dimensionality (2048-d vs. 16-d), and training recipe (lr, augmentation — `REPOSITORY_MAP.md` §2.8) — on top of n=1 checkpoint, seed 42 only. Already correctly downgraded to "categorical reference, descriptive only" in the latest working turn; this review is recording that the downgrade needs to hold in the actual manuscript, not just in chat.
3. **Any claim baseline_soft's *geometry* sits inside/outside the ladder's range (E1b/E2b's own stated descriptive comparison).** `e1_geometry_metrics.csv` has **zero** `baseline_soft` rows. E1b was specified in `experiment_contract.md` and never executed. There is currently no data to make this comparison at all, in either direction.
4. **Any generalization claim beyond the ISIC/PAD-UFES pair.** E3 (HAM10000) was never attempted — not even data acquisition. `REPOSITORY_MAP.md` already documents that ISIC2018 and HAM10000 overlap heavily, which additionally means E3 cannot be run as currently specified ("strictly held-out third domain," `SPEC.md` §4) without a verified deduplication step that doesn't exist as working code anywhere in either source repo.
5. **Any generalization claim beyond CSG-Lite's specific architecture.** E4 (DST-Skin) was never attempted.
6. **Implicit treatment of ISIC-test/PAD-UFES AUROC as a clean OOD measurement.** `threats_to_validity.md` #1 documents that PAD-UFES is *also* CSG's own auxiliary training-domain stream — every checkpoint has seen these "OOD" images during training. This threat is unresolved project-wide and is *more* consequential for E2.6/E2.7 specifically than it was for the original E1↔E2 design — see §6.i.
7. **Fisher ratio's role in any E1/E2/E2.6 interpretation.** `fisher_ratio_defense.md` §4 and `open_questions.md` Q2 both state, explicitly, that the Corr(κ, J)-at-fixed-geometry bootstrap check is a precondition "before E2 interprets any Fisher-ratio result." That check was never run. In this specific dataset Fisher ratio didn't end up driving the headline claim (condition_number did), which limits the damage, but the pre-registered gate was still never checked before results were interpreted.
8. **Checkpoint provenance ("this is the best-val checkpoint").** `threats_to_validity.md` #3 flags that verifying "best" requires cross-checking logged validation metrics, which aren't confirmed present. In practice, every checkpoint used so far is simply *the only file in its seed directory* — that rules out the specific *directory-mtime-picks-wrong-file* bug already found elsewhere in CSG-SKin, but it is not the same thing as an independent verification that training itself selected this epoch as best-val.
9. **The residual half of Q1** (whether `runA_grl`/`runB_orth1`/`runB` share an identical lr/transform recipe with each other, not just differing from `baseline_soft`) was flagged in `open_questions.md` as something that "should still be spot-checked" — and never was. The entire primary ladder's causal reading (geometry change ← λ_orth alone) rests on this being true.

## 6. Places where reviewers will attack

**i. The PAD-UFES-as-adversarial-target circularity (the sharpest, least-discussed risk in the project).** `runA_grl`/`runB_orth1`/`runB` are trained with a domain-adversarial (GRL) objective, and PAD-UFES is the very domain that objective is trained to make indistinguishable from ISIC. The paper's headline E2.6/E2.7 finding — domain membership is decodable but not distance-accessible — is measured on *exactly* the domain the training objective targeted. A statistically literate reviewer will ask: is this a general property of disentangled representations, or the close-to-definitional consequence of testing a domain-adversarial model against the specific domain it was adversarially trained on? Nothing in the current evidence distinguishes these two readings. This needs to be either addressed directly (e.g., discuss why decodability surviving *at all* under an adversarial objective targeting exactly this domain is itself non-obvious) or explicitly scoped as a limitation — silence here is the single biggest risk to the novelty claim in item 3.

**ii. The baseline-vs-CSG confound**, already caught internally: architecture + dimensionality + training recipe all differ at once, n=1. If the manuscript's abstract or Discussion leans on this table even rhetorically ("for reference, a standard baseline did not show this gap, consistent with..."), a reviewer will name all three confounded variables in one sentence and the paper's strongest visual (the 2×2 table) becomes its weakest paragraph.

**iii. HAM10000 was never run, and the source repos' own documentation (`REPOSITORY_MAP.md`) already states it overlaps heavily with ISIC2018.** If E3 is mentioned at all as planned/future work, "strictly held-out" (current text in `SPEC.md` §4) cannot survive review as written.

**iv. Small, uneven n.** 13 total (rung, seed) points, `runB` at n=3 vs. 5 for the other two rungs. The permutation-test machinery here is unusually rigorous (full-enumeration exact p-values, not scipy's silently-inapplicable asymptotic fallback — a real, citable methodological strength) — but rigor of the test doesn't fix the sample size. Non-significant τ's (e.g., every metric vs. AUROC) are weak evidence of "no relationship" at n=13, not strong evidence, and a reviewer will say so.

**v. No matched non-disentangled control at the same architecture/dimensionality.** The one checkpoint family that would directly test "is this disentanglement-specific or just an artifact of 16-d bottlenecks in general" — a non-adversarially-trained 16-d CSG-Lite bottleneck — does not exist anywhere in the checkpoint inventory. This is the single most valuable missing experiment for the paper's actual novelty claim (see §9), and its absence is also the reason (ii) above cannot currently be fixed with existing data.

**vi. Multiple comparisons, uncorrected.** 5 geometry metrics × 2 experiments, 5 scorers, 3 probes — no Bonferroni/FDR anywhere. `geometry_metric_audit.md` raises multiple comparisons as a reason to cut candidate metrics from 13 to 3 (`internal_review.md` already flagged this as inconsistent — cutting metrics on multiple-comparisons grounds while never correcting the comparisons that remain).

**vii. Below-chance, not merely at-chance, AUROC.** Worth a reviewer's specific question: an OOD detector at AUROC≈0.5 says "no signal, as expected under successful domain invariance." AUROC≈0.40, consistently, says something more specific and stranger — PAD-UFES looks *more* typical than ISIC's own held-out test set by these distance metrics. The project has good supporting diagnostics here (NV-attractor, norm) but no mechanistic account of *why below chance specifically*, and a reviewer will ask for one.

**viii. Construct validity of "reliability estimation."** Never operationally defined anywhere in the project's own documents (already flagged in `internal_review.md`, never revisited since no manuscript text exists to revisit it in). Is it OOD-AUROC specifically, calibration, selective prediction? The whole paper is currently built on OOD-AUROC standing in for the broader construct without saying so.

## 7. Missing citations

- **Sun et al. 2022** (k-NN OOD detection) — used as a method (E2.6), referred to only as "Sun et al. 2022-style" in code comments and design docs; no bibliography exists anywhere in the repo.
- **Lee et al. 2018** (Mahalanobis OOD detection) — same situation; invoked by name in prose, never formally cited.
- **Ganin & Lempitsky 2015** (gradient reversal / domain-adversarial training) — foundational for `runA_grl`'s own mechanism; currently uncited anywhere.
- **Papyan, Han & Donoho 2020** (Neural Collapse, PNAS) — already flagged in `internal_review.md` as the obvious, currently-missing framework for the within/between-class covariance-geometry question Fisher ratio and condition number are built around.
- **Ma et al. 2018** (local intrinsic dimensionality, ICLR) — already flagged in `internal_review.md`; a per-point geometric OOD diagnostic more directly relevant than several 1980s clustering-validity indices the audit did include.
- **Alain & Bengio 2016 / Hewitt & Liang 2019** (linear probing methodology, and the probing-vs-selectivity critique). This is not a nice-to-have: Hewitt & Liang's core point — that a probe finding decodable information doesn't establish that the original model *uses* that information — is directly relevant to how strongly item 3's novelty claim can be stated, and its absence is a gap a probing-literature-aware reviewer will notice immediately.
- **Geirhos et al. 2020** (shortcut learning) — motivates the entire premise (CSG-SKin exists to mitigate shortcuts); currently unc­ited.
- **Cover 1965** (linear separability in high dimensions) — would directly strengthen the paper's own admission that the baseline's 2048-d probe/distance numbers are not proof of anything disentanglement-specific.
- **Hendrycks & Gimpel 2017** — standard AUROC/FPR95 OOD-benchmark convention; currently used without attribution.

## 8. Missing figures

- **No figure for the paper's own best evidence.** The ladder-internal distance-vs-probe contrast (`table_e2_7_domain_vs_distance.csv`) is the single most important number in the project and currently exists only as a CSV row. It needs its own figure — likely a grouped bar or paired-dot plot, distance-scorer AUROC vs. probe AUROC, one group per rung, with the baseline_soft point visually and typographically distinguished (e.g., grayed out / separate panel / explicit "descriptive reference, not a controlled comparison" annotation) so a reader's eye doesn't average it in with the clean ladder evidence.
- **No schematic/causal-graph figure.** Given how much the mechanistic framing has moved (Mahalanobis-fails → all-scorers-fail → decodability-≠-accessibility) across this project's own history, a simple Figure 1 laying out the experimental logic (ladder → geometry metrics → distance scorers → probe) would orient a BSPC reader who has not lived through that evolution and has only the final manuscript.
- **No baseline_soft geometry panel** (blocked on E1b never having been run — see §9).
- **No embedding visualization of any kind**, by explicit, reasonable authorial choice ("no pretty picture without a hypothesis"). Defensible, but worth naming explicitly in Methods/Limitations rather than leaving a reader to wonder why a paper about representation geometry never shows the representation. A narrowly-scoped exception — visualizing only the linear probe's own discriminant direction, a specific quantity already computed, not an unguided UMAP tour — would satisfy both the authors' own rule and a typical reviewer's expectation.

## 9. Missing experiments

- **E1b** (`baseline_soft` geometry: condition number, Fisher ratio, Mardia kurtosis) — specified, never run. `e1_geometry_metrics.csv` has zero such rows.
- **E3** (HAM10000 replication) — never attempted; blocked on data acquisition and on a verified dedup against ISIC2018's already-documented heavy overlap.
- **E4** (DST-Skin cross-architecture check) — never attempted; the only planned generalization check beyond CSG-Lite.
- **Fisher-ratio/condition-number entanglement bootstrap** (`fisher_ratio_defense.md` §4) — designed as a precondition, never run.
- **Checkpoint best-val cross-verification against real training logs** — never done for any of the 13+ checkpoints in use.
- **A matched, non-adversarial, same-architecture, same-16-d control.** Does not exist in the checkpoint inventory. This is the single highest-value missing experiment: it is the only thing that could directly separate "disentanglement causes this" from "16-d bottlenecks cause this" without the current baseline confound.
- **A direct check of the PAD-UFES-as-adversarial-target circularity** (item 6.i) — e.g., whether the same decodability/accessibility gap appears on a domain the model was *not* adversarially trained against (this is exactly what E3 would have provided, had it been run — its absence is doubly costly for this reason specifically, not just for generic "third domain" hygiene).
- **A formal significance test for domain-probe AUROC against 0.5** (currently point estimates + CV only — likely significant given effect size and n, but never actually tested).
- **Any multiple-comparisons correction** across the ~15+ statistical tests already run.

## 10. Strength score: **7/10**

The CSG-internal comparison (E2.6, single architecture, single dimensionality, three qualitatively different distance scorers, all failing identically) is genuinely 8–9/10 work — clean, well-controlled, and backed by an unusually rigorous statistical pipeline (verified exact-permutation tests, independently-checked scorer implementations, honest documentation of every reversal and correction along the way). The score is pulled down by: several explicitly pre-registered gating checks that were never executed (Fisher entanglement, checkpoint provenance, Q1's residual spot-check), one central planned experiment (E3) never attempted despite being load-bearing for the generalization claim, and the total absence of manuscript prose to assess argument construction, which is itself part of "strength" for a submission-readiness review.

## 11. Novelty score: **conditional, 5–7/10**

7/10 if the PAD-UFES-adversarial-target circularity (§6.i) is addressed or credibly neutralized in Discussion, and the baseline comparison stays demoted to illustrative-only as currently intended. Closer to 5/10 if that circularity is left unaddressed — a reviewer who raises it can reduce "a previously overlooked general failure mode of disentangled representations" to "an expected consequence of testing a domain-adversarial model against its own adversarial target," which is a real, defensible, and much less novel reading of the identical numbers.

BSPC acceptance probability: **not yet assessable as a submission; conditionally ~40–55% with major revision if written to the standard already set by this project's own internal audits, ~15–20% if the confounded baseline table or "strictly held-out" HAM10000 language survives into the actual draft.**

There is no manuscript to score against BSPC's actual bar. Scoring the *science* as if fairly written up: BSPC's typical standard (solid empirical rigor, application-grounded, tolerant of a narrower theoretical contribution than a top ML venue would demand) fits this project's actual strength — the clean ladder-internal result — reasonably well. The swing factors are entirely under the authors' control at this point: (a) keep the baseline comparison demoted and explicitly caveated, not promoted to headline evidence; (b) address the PAD-UFES circularity directly rather than by omission; (c) strike "strictly held-out" and scope E3/E4 explicitly as future work; (d) add the missing bibliography and the one missing headline figure; (e) either run the Fisher-ratio entanglement check or state plainly in Limitations that it was pre-registered and not completed. None of these require new GPU experiments except the optional matched-control run (§9) — most are writing and disclosure discipline, which this project has already demonstrated, in its own internal documents, that it is capable of.
